### `AICM-L2-PREF-103` — Durable Change Case identity
- fidelity: verbatim
- источник: `11_SESSION_18_DECISION_REGISTER.md` §4; `02_…PREFERENCE_DRAFT_v0.1.md` §13 Confirmed preference map
- текст: **Status:** CONFIRMED. **Decision:** Change Case is a stable AICM-owned identity for one bounded change objective. It survives session, worker, provider, plan and attempt replacement and remains distinct from the Durable Program executing the work, the Capability being changed and every target object. **Boundary:** A Change Case does not itself grant permission to modify, promote, activate or expose any target.
  - **расширение draft:**
    Architecture theorem (draft §3):

    ```text
    Need != Claim != Candidate != Change Set != Assessment
    Promotion != Activation != Stabilization != Outcome
    proof != authority
    impact discovery != authority
    restriction != permanent freeze
    rollback != universal default
    Session End != Change End
    ```

    AICM owns the change lineage. Replaceable providers perform roles. Evidence supports assessment. Provenance-matched authorities permit promotion and activation. Consumer/Program reconciliation protects continuity. Bounded protection preserves both safety and a feasible path for continued evolution.

    Core change objects (draft §4):

    | Object | Meaning | Does not imply |
    |---|---|---|
    | Change Case | durable identity of one bounded change objective | authority or success |
    | Change Need | evidence-bearing reason to investigate | preferred solution |
    | Change Claim | falsifiable intended effect and protected boundaries | admitted Candidate |
    | Change Candidate | one proposed answer to the Claim | proof or promotion |
    | Change Set Edition | exact immutable successor composition | activation |
    | Impact Discovery Cut | exact state against which impact is assessed | graph-owned authority |
    | Change Assessment | bounded evaluation over exact evidence | promotion authority |
    | Promotion Disposition | eligibility for bounded progression | consumer exposure |
    | Activation Disposition | permission to bind exact successor/scope | stable success |
    | Stabilization Disposition | post-activation conclusion for declared scope | global validity |
    | Restriction/Rollback/Recovery Disposition | protective or restorative action | erased failure history |
    | Change Outcome | separated effects and consequences | automatic knowledge truth |

    End-to-end semantic flow (draft §5):

    ```text
    trigger / outcome / gap / obligation
    → Change Need
    → Change Claim
    → competing Change Candidates
    → exact Change Set Edition
    → exact Impact Discovery Cut
    → evidence production
    → independent strongest challenge
    → Change Assessment
    → Promotion Disposition
    → consumer / Program / dependency / migration / recovery reconciliation
    → Activation Disposition
    → shadow / canary / restricted activation
    → monitored actual use and consequences
    → stabilization, restriction, recovery, rollback, revalidation or successor
    → Change Terminal Closed
    → exact next Evolution Portfolio cursor
    ```

### `AICM-L2-PREF-142` — Progress-preserving restriction, rollback, recovery and revalidation
- fidelity: verbatim
- источник: `11_SESSION_18_DECISION_REGISTER.md` §4; `02_…PREFERENCE_DRAFT_v0.1.md` §13 Confirmed preference map
- текст: **Status:** CONFIRMED. **Decision:** Protective action is least-restrictive, local, scope-bound, time/budget-bound and evidence-bearing. AICM may restrict exposure, stop a route, use fallback, place a Program in typed WAIT, contain, recover or roll back to an exact known state. Rollback is one option rather than a universal default; irreversible change uses containment, compensation and recovery successor semantics. Safe predecessor service and a parallel successor-evidence path are preserved where feasible. **Boundary:** A failed Candidate or protective restriction cannot poison unrelated or successor changes, create a permanent global freeze, reset evidence budgets or erase failure history. Persistent blockage itself creates a Change Need and liveness review.
  - **расширение draft:**
    Safe-progress rule (draft §10):

    Safety mechanisms must protect without becoming a self-sealing ban on improvement. Restrictions are least-restrictive, local, time/budget-bound and reviewable. A failed Candidate does not permanently block a successor. Where feasible, AICM retains a safe predecessor and a parallel evidence path. Persistent gate blockage creates its own Change Need.

    Guardrail 04 specializes AICM-L2-PREF-125, 128, 134, 137, 141–146 (не отдельный PREF ID):

    Governing rule: «Protection must reduce the exact threatened consequence while preserving the safest feasible route to continued learning, successor evidence and future activation.»

    Least-restrictive protective ladder:
    ```text
    increase observation
    → reduce rate/scope
    → restrict one consumer or route
    → switch provider/fallback
    → typed Program WAIT or replan
    → contain affected state
    → suspend exact enrollment
    → exact rollback where a real predecessor exists
    → recovery successor / compensation for irreversible change
    ```
    The ladder is not mechanically sequential. Consequence and urgency may justify a stronger step, but the selected scope and rationale remain explicit.

    A failure, restriction or rollback applies only to the exact affected Candidate, successor, consumer, Program, dependency or authority contour unless impact evidence proves wider propagation.

    It does not automatically:

    - invalidate unrelated Capabilities;
    - freeze the Evolution Portfolio;
    - block all future Candidates for the target;
    - erase a valid stable predecessor;
    - reset previous evidence or budgets;
    - require Owner approval for later autonomous progression.

    Where consequence and resources allow, AICM preserves:

    - a last-known-good predecessor or bounded fallback for continuity;
    - a separate shadow/simulation/evidence path for the successor;
    - exact comparison and migration evidence;
    - an independent route to resolve the blocking gap.

    A protection decision is therefore not equivalent to abandoning improvement.

    Every required gate must have at least one semantically feasible satisfaction path, bounded terminal disposition or named external predicate. Demanding impossible certainty, unavailable evidence without an alternative, or circular proof is a gate defect.

    Signals of a liveness defect include:

    - repeated Candidates blocked by the same non-material gap;
    - a restriction with no expiry, budget, next wake or revalidation path;
    - repeated rollback without learning return;
    - evaluator or policy criteria changing after each success;
    - a required predecessor that the architecture itself prevents from existing;
    - persistent safe shadow evidence that can never reach bounded activation.

    A liveness defect creates a new Change Need against the gate/policy itself and enters the same governed change process under a stricter anti-self-modification contour.

    Rollback is valid only when an exact restorable predecessor and state relationship exist. For externally irreversible or information-losing changes, AICM uses containment, compensation, reconstruction and recovery-successor semantics and declares residual loss.

    The word rollback may not hide irreversibility.

    After restriction or recovery:

    - the exact affected proof and enrollments become revalidation-required;
    - failure evidence remains attached;
    - a materially different Candidate receives its own assessment rather than inheriting permanent rejection;
    - repeated failure may strengthen evidence requirements but cannot create an undefined permanent ban;
    - stable predecessor use does not imply that improvement work is closed.

    Fast protective narrowing may be pre-delegated. Re-expansion remains subject to the exact activation gate. This asymmetry protects consequences without converting protection into routine Owner control.
